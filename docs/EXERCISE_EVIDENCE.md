# Exercise evidence review (S14)

Every exercise in `Analysis/Exercises.swift` has a `basis` (strength, one line, citations) that's shown in the run view under **Why this exercise**. This page is the review behind those notes. It's meant to be handed to a speech-language pathologist (SLP) to check: the app must not call itself "SLP-approved" until one has.

**How the sources were checked:** Davies et al. 2015 was read in full (open-access copy on Zenodo). For the other sources, abstracts and indexed records were checked for the claim made and for the citation details. Kapsner-Smith 2015, Carew 2007, Hancock 2014 and Kawitzky 2020 should get a full-text check by the SLP reviewer.

**Strength labels**
- **Research-supported:** tested in published studies, usually small ones.
- **Recognised clinical practice:** described in clinical guidelines and protocols, with little direct testing.
- **Limited research:** related findings only, labelled as such in the app.

| Exercise | Stages | Strength | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up (breath, hum, trills, siren, yawn-sigh) | all | Research-supported | Semi-occluded vocal tract exercises (Titze 2006; RCT, Kapsner-Smith et al. 2015). Yawn-sigh widens the pharynx on endoscopy (Boone & McFarlane 1993). The breathing step is common practice with little direct evidence; the app says so. |
| Easy hums into words (new) | 1 | Research-supported | Resonant voice therapy: hum into words and phrases (Verdolini-Marston et al. 1995); a systematic review graded the evidence as moderate (Yiu, Lo & Barrett 2017). |
| Pitch slides | 1, 2 | Research-supported | Glides from Vocal Function Exercises (Stemple et al. 1994, RCT); glides on semi-occluded sounds through the middle and upper range (Davies et al. 2015, citing Titze). |
| Hold target pitch | 2, 4 | Recognised clinical practice | A training pitch produced without strain, held and then extended; start near the habitual pitch and raise it gradually (Davies et al. 2015, citing Mordaunt 2012 and Dacakis 2012). Sustained vowels are in VFE. This is the basis for the stage 2 stepped target. |
| Resonance: forward ↔ back (replaces "big dog / small dog") | 3 | Research-supported | Forward tongue carriage and slight lip spreading raised F1–F3 (Carew, Dacakis & Oates 2007); listed in Davies et al. 2015. Big dog / small dog (panting, larynx height) comes from online voice-training communities with no published support, so it was reframed rather than kept. |
| Vowel ladder (ee/ah) | 3, 4, 6 | Research-supported | /i/ as the model mouth shape for other vowels (Davies et al. 2015, citing Mordaunt 2012); visual formant biofeedback shifted F2 in a small study (Kawitzky & McAllister 2020). |
| Words and phrases (new) | 4, 5 | Recognised clinical practice | Hierarchy from sounds to words to longer utterances; everyday words (hi, bye, yes, no) and real-life phrases for carryover (Davies et al. 2015). |
| Reading: Rainbow Passage | 5, 6 | Recognised clinical practice | Standard passage (Fairbanks 1960); reading is the step between phrases and conversation. |
| Intonation (new) | 5 | Research-supported (perception) | More upward glides and a wider semitone range linked with female perception (Hancock, Colton & Douglas 2014). Other studies found narrower ranges read as more feminine (Owen & Hancock 2010, via Davies et al. 2015). The app records the range but sets no target. |
| Lighter ↔ heavier (new) | 6 | **Limited research** | "Vocal weight" is mainly a voice-training idea. A model study found spectral shape has a moderate effect on gender perception (Zhang, Zhang & Kreiman 2022). Breathiness findings are mixed (Leung, Oates & Chan 2018). No drill has been tested directly. The instructions warn never to press or squeeze. |
| Cool-down | all | **Limited research** | Singers report benefit, but objective measures haven't shown a clear effect (Ragan 2016). It uses the same SOVT sounds as the warm-up. |

## Safety wording kept in every exercise
Comfortable volume; stop if anything hurts or feels scratchy; strain alerts and the daily cap stay on. Davies et al. 2015 (Summary of Recommendations, item 4) advises against self-guided voice change without at least one professional assessment. The Exercises tab now says so under the stage path.

## Progression notes
- **Stage 2 steps** move 1 semitone from the baseline towards the goal midpoint after the hold drill passes (no strain alert) on 2 separate days at that step. The stage is ready when the step reaches the goal and passes on 3 days.
- **Stage 5 intonation** uses an exercise-count criterion, not a semitone target, because the evidence points both ways. This differs from PROGRESSION.md ("pitch variation near the target").
- **Strain** holds every stage: any strain alert, or a day at the cap, in the last 3 days leaves the "ready" checks unmet.
- History stage markers (PROGRESSION.md) were not built in S14. They're left for S16.
